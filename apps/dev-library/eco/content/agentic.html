2. The surface defined

2.1 What the Agentic Design Surface is

The Agentic Design Surface is the design system for non-human actors: software agents that discover, negotiate, transact, and settle across the UZE ecosystem on behalf of a principal — a person, a desk, or an enterprise — who sets the mandate and remains accountable for it.

A human using UZEmarkets reads pages, weighs judgments, and taps approvals. An agent using UZEmarkets does none of that. It connects to a machine surface, presents a wallet-bound identity, states what it is authorized to do, calls tools, pays per call, and leaves a complete record. The design problem is therefore different in kind from the human surface:

  • A human surface must be legible — a person must understand it at a glance.
  • An agent surface must be bounded — an agent must be unable to exceed its mandate, and every boundary must be enforced by the system, not requested of the agent.
  • A human surface earns trust by presentation.
  • An agent surface earns trust by record — identity, gating, and an audit trail that a principal can inspect after the fact and rely on.

Every rule in this document follows from those four verbs — discover, negotiate, transact, settle — and from one constraint that governs all of them: the agent acts; the principal answers for it; the system proves what happened.

2.2 The pairing with the Human Design Surface

The two surfaces are one system seen from two sides, joined by the dual-surface contract:

  • The console is a view over the API. Anything a human sees in a console exists because the underlying machine surface produced it. There is no human-only back door and no agent-only shadow system.
  • Autonomous-first for money and execution; human-first for judgment. Agents execute inside mandates without waiting for a person. Decisions that are judgment — approving an assessment's validity, accepting an engagement, ruling on a claim — are reserved to humans by construction, not by policy. In the Talent system, two human gates stand before any assessment runs, and an agent identity can satisfy neither gate. That is the contract working as designed.
  • One chronicle under both. A human's approval and an agent's call are events in the same record, so a principal reviewing a console sees the whole history of a matter — human and machine actions interleaved, in order.

The HUMAN ECOSYSTEM DESIGN (ratified by James 2026-11-19) governs how all of this looks and reads to people: the tokens, the wordmark, the car wash every page passes before it deploys. The AGENTIC ECOSYSTEM DESIGN governs how all of it behaves for machines. Neither is complete without the other; a surface that is beautiful to a human and unbounded for an agent is a liability, and a surface that is rigorous for an agent and illegible to its principal is unaccountable.

2.3 The frame: optimizing enterprise innovation

This surface exists for one purpose, stated as James directed it: optimizing enterprise innovation.

Enterprises do not innovate by adding dashboards. They innovate when work that used to wait on a person — sourcing, matching, underwriting, allocating, settling — can proceed continuously, inside limits they set, with proof they can audit. The agentic surface is what makes that possible inside the UZE ecosystem:

  • Discoverable — an enterprise's agents can find the ecosystem's desks and tools through the same directories and tagging the agents themselves use (§3.1, §3.8).
  • Gated — no agent acts outside a pre-authorized mandate; limits are set by the principal before the first call, and enforced before any call executes (§3.3).
  • Auditable — every call is a chronicle event; the record is the product's memory and the principal's evidence (§3.4).
  • Settle-able — value moves on a stated rail law, the same law for a human-initiated payment and an agent-initiated one (§3.7).

An enterprise that can send its agents into a market with those four properties can experiment at machine speed without surrendering control. That is the innovation this surface is designed to optimize — and it is why the treasury domain in Addendum B, Part 2, is the ultimate focus: an enterprise's own balances are the first place this discipline pays for itself.

3. How it works

3.1 MCP-first surfaces — the agent door

The ecosystem's agent door is the Model Context Protocol (MCP). Every capability offered to agents is offered as an MCP tool: named, described in one canonical language, tier-gated, and metered.

UZE Instant MCP is the platform's single endpoint (mcp.uzemarkets.com). It comes in two tiers:

  • UZE Instance — the generic tier: branded compute plus settlement, the nine base tools, no vertical access.
  • UZE Markets Instance — the premium tier: the base tools plus the vertical pathways into the ecosystem's desks and markets.

The catalog of record is 18 tools — 9 base + 9 vertical. The base tools cover the instance lifecycle (provision, credentials, status, terminate), balance, and payment (pay_via_x402); the escrow pair (create_escrow, release_milestone) belongs to the settlement phase. The vertical tools open the desks — including the three Talent tools (submit_assessment, score_assessment, publish_assessment_outcome), which are live.

Distribution is directories. Agents find tools where agents look: the LLM and MCP directories. The filed strategy is two-tier — a conversion tier first (the Anthropic MCP registry, then Smithery, then Cursor/Windsurf), a reach tier after (OpenAI, Perplexity, DeepSeek, Venice, Kimi, Hostinger, and the open MCP directories). One descriptive language serves the listings, the site's MCP section, and the Knowledge Base identically, so no surface invents its own wording. No listing is claimed anywhere until it is verified live — the standing rule is that listings claim only what is green.

3.2 Wallet-bound agent identity

An agent's identity is its wallet. Sign-in to the Instant MCP is a wallet signature: the signature is the credential, the wallet is the account, and every authorization hangs off that binding. There is no separate agent username to issue, leak, or revoke — revoking the wallet's authorization revokes the agent.

This is the root of the trust model. A principal can answer, at any time: which wallet acted, under whose pre-authorization, within what limits, and what exactly it did — because identity, limits, and record all hang off the same binding.

3.3 Three-layer gating — enforcement before execution

No call executes until it has passed three layers, in order:

  1. User pre-authorization. The principal sets the fence in advance: per-call and per-day ceilings, and optionally a tool allowlist. This layer is the mandate, stated by the human or enterprise that owns the wallet.
  2. MCP enforcement. The endpoint checks every call against the fence before execution — not after, not on a sampling basis. A call outside the fence is refused at the boundary, and the refusal itself is recorded.
  3. Vertical gates. Behind individual tools stand the vertical's own gates. Some are mechanical (tier, balance). Some are human by construction: the Talent vertical's two gates — an engaging employer's confirmation of intent, and an approver of record's confirmation of validity — cannot be satisfied by any agent identity, at any tier, ever.

Revocation cascades at the same boundary: when a principal revokes, sessions die, later calls refuse, and an in-flight call completes — a clean edge, demonstrated in the Phase 1 proofs.

3.4 The chronicle — every call an event

Every call, grant, and refusal is written to an append-only chronicle. The chronicle is not a log file kept for debugging; it is a designed component of the surface — the shared record under both the human console and the agent endpoint (§2.2), and the raw material of every audit, report, and performance claim the ecosystem will ever make about agent activity.

Its current limit is stated plainly in §4 and Addendum A: the chronicle is a record, not yet a proven tamper-proof record. Closing that gap is Pillar 3 work.

3.5 x402 metering — payment as a protocol act

Agent activity is metered with x402: payment is part of the call, not a monthly invoice reconstructed afterward. An agent pays per call from its wallet, inside its pre-authorized ceilings, and the payment is itself a chronicle event. Metering does three kinds of work at once: it prices the surface, it rate-limits by economics rather than by courtesy, and it makes every unit of agent activity carry its own cost record — the data-science substrate for understanding what agents actually do in the ecosystem, which the agents-first principle requires be observed with best practices, not guessed at.

3.6 Settlement — one rail law

Value settled through the agentic surface follows the same rail law as everything else in the ecosystem (James, 2026-11-18). There is no separate agent rail:

  1. USDC on Solana is the default for everything.
  2. Cascade, only if the prior rail is unavailable: USDC on Base →
  3. USDG on Robinhood Chain →
  4. Tempo/Stripe CASH — only if none of the first three is available, and never a default.

Two carve-outs are part of the law:

  • CASH is excluded from SigLayer and TradeLayer entirely — those surfaces settle on the first three rails only.
  • Inside the World.xyz/AEInfo vertical, CASH is the required default — the one vertical where the cascade starts at the fourth rail, because that vertical's own settlement asset requires it.

Two role definitions keep the law intelligible. UZE counts and credits usefulness — measurement and attribution of the usefulness an offering delivered. UZE is the brand and the count/credit unit: not a token, not money, never a payment method. UZD is a reward only — it accrues from counted, credited activity as an incentive and attribution instrument. No UZD ledger exists yet, and nothing in this document claims one.

3.7 Discovery through tagging — SEO and GEO for agents

The agents-first principle extends past the endpoint: agent engagement through app tagging for SEO and GEO, and savvy, strategic development of social-feed-bound content with optimal execution practices across social and messaging channels (James, 2026-11-18, 22:43 ET extension).

In practice: the ecosystem's pages and listings are tagged so that search engines, generative engines, and agent frameworks can find, parse, and correctly describe what each surface does — the dual-surface discipline the HIADR Content Desk already embodies (every post exists as human analysis and as machine-readable structure with transact endpoints). An agent that cannot discover a desk cannot use it; discovery is therefore a designed layer of this surface, not a marketing afterthought. The directory strategy (§3.1) and the tagging discipline are the same strategy at two depths: be listed where agents look, and be legible to whatever comes looking.

3.8 Two MCP families — never blended

The ecosystem's MCPs fall into two families, and the families are kept apart by rule (James):

  • Product MCPs — UZE Underwrite (the Autonomous Underwriting desk), UZE Assign (the DealFi matching desk), UZE Auction (sealed-bid secondary auctions). These face the ecosystem's desks and markets.
  • Trading MCPs — TradeLayer and SigLayer. These face trading and settlement.

The Brokerage MCP is the TradeLayer door, built on the Robinhood model: a user connects their own LLM to their brokerage accounts through a dedicated, separately funded account, with every order reviewed before it executes. Crypto held in Robinhood custody is Robinhood's, and is labelled as theirs — never presented as UZE's crypto solution. The build order is fixed: Robinhood MCP first, then TradeLayer launches with it, then SigLayer as full production once TradeLayer works.

The matching desks come online in their own order — DealFi (DFI) first, then Fundio (FVC) — under the line that names them: Autonomous Underwriting, Talent & DealFi matching desks — UZEmarkets superIntelligence.

5. Addendum A — Pillars of the Agentic Design Surface

A.1 Fundamental Pillar 1 — Agentic trading harnesses and terminals

The decision loop: an agent holds a persistent objective, does its own research, sizes positions, executes, and monitors risk, while the human moves from operator to principal — setting the mandate, not placing the trades.

What UZE has. The harness-shaped pieces exist: the MCP utility (a connect-your-own-LLM terminal surface with a dedicated, separately funded account and review-before-action), the Instant MCP endpoint with its gating and chronicle, and the matching desks as decision-loop infrastructure. The form factor is right: intent in, fenced execution out, everything on the record.

What is missing. The harness is not trading-hardened — no dynamic position sizing, no take-profit/stop-loss discipline, no backtest-before-live-money mode (the exact gap the Coinbase Ventures mapping names; the remedy is scoped in Addendum B, Part 1). The brokerage door runs on demo data; there is no live brokerage, no retention or volume evidence, and no demonstrated conversion of a user's stated intent into a working portfolio. Intent-as-a-Service is the direction this pillar points in — per the value-accrual mapping, owning the decision loop is where value concentrates — but it is a direction, not a product.

A.2 Fundamental Pillar 2 — Market reasoning models and data assets — a real edge

Intelligence is the layer that decides whether the harness's actions are worth taking. The durable asset is proprietary data and the reasoning built on it — the "context loop" in which use improves the model that serves the next use.

What UZE has. Two genuine data assets. The QuantGPT data plane serves the Delphi information-markets program. UZE Underwrite's same-micro-region benchmarks are a proprietary, defensible asset: financial claims (Assuraties) are judged only against benchmarks from the same micro-region, inside a fixed seven-bucket P&L taxonomy — Revenue; Operating Expenses; Taxes; Insurance(s); Interest Expense/Income; Miscellaneous Net Expenses/Income; Profit (Net Income) — because taxes, the largest driver of micro-market variability, make cross-region comparison meaningless. Claims that withstand the process are Assuratized: reliance-grade, with the evidence trail attached, served through the Reliance API. The HIADR taxonomy and the Content Desk data layer add structured, dual-surface market data on top.

What is missing. No purpose-built market reasoning model; no simulated-trading environment in which models or strategies could be trained and tested; no verified strategy marketplace; no machine-payable data product behind the x402 payment tool — the payment rail exists, the data product behind it does not yet. The context-loop flywheel is designed, not evidenced: there is no backtested performance to show, and this document claims none.

A.3 Fundamental Pillar 3 — Identity, audit trails, and performance-verification infrastructure for non-human financial decision-makers

When the decision-maker is not human, trust cannot rest on reputation or relationship. It rests on infrastructure: provable identity, a complete audit trail, and verified performance that a counterparty can check without asking permission.

What UZE has. This is the most built-out pillar. Wallet-bound agent identity (§3.2); three-layer gating with enforcement before execution (§3.3); the append-only chronicle under every call (§3.4); the revoke cascade at the call boundary; AssurativeV2's evidence trails, in which a claim's path from Assuraties to Assuratized is itself a verifiable record; and the Talent vertical's demonstration that some gates are human by construction. The pattern across all of it: an agent's authority is provable, bounded, and revocable, and its history is inspectable.

What is missing. The chronicle is a record, not a proven tamper-proof record — no cryptographic anchoring has been demonstrated. There is no verified performance tracking for agents (no public, checkable record of what an agent's decisions returned), no reputation system, and no "Know Your Agent" attestation product. Regulatory literacy is a design stance, not a filed compliance product. Until performance verification exists, Pillar 3 proves what an agent did but not yet how well it did it — and the second is what counterparties will ultimately price.

A.4 Supporting pillars

The three Fundamental Pillars stand on five supporting disciplines, each already stated in §3 and collected here because the surface fails without any one of them:

  1. Wallet-bound identity — the wallet is the credential; authorization, limits, and history hang off one binding (§3.2).
  2. Every call a chronicle event — no silent actions; grants and refusals are recorded with the same care as executions (§3.4).
  3. Gating before execution — the fence is checked before the call runs, never audited after the fact as a substitute (§3.3).
  4. Settlement per rail law — one cascade for humans and agents alike, with its two carve-outs stated, never improvised (§3.6).
  5. Discovery through tagging — listings and GEO/SEO tagging as a designed layer, so agents can find and correctly describe the surface (§3.7).

6. Addendum B — Two domains, kept separate

This addendum covers the two financial domains the agentic surface touches. They are different domains, and they must never be blended — the same rule the ecosystem applies to its MCP families applies here, one level up:

  • Digital Asset Trading is about positions: maximizing gains, optimizing position sizing, setting risk tolerance. It has nothing to do with corporate or entity cash flow.
  • Digital Asset Treasury is about balances: the cash-flow and fiat balances an entity holds, and how those balances are worked while they are not needed. It has nothing to do with taking trading positions.

Different money, different purpose, different risk, different customer conversation. Part 1 describes the trading domain — and states plainly that it is not the ultimate focus. Part 2 describes the treasury domain — the ultimate focus, and the center of the enterprise-innovation frame in §2.3.

Part 1 — Digital Asset Trading

What this domain is. Trading activity conducted through agents: a principal states an objective and a risk tolerance; a harness researches, sizes positions, executes, and manages exits. The design questions are the trader's questions — how much, how sized, where the stop is, what the backtest said — asked of a machine that must answer them inside a fence.

What exists. The TradeLayer/SigLayer family is the trading surface (§3.8), sequenced behind the Brokerage MCP door: Robinhood MCP first, TradeLayer with it, SigLayer as full production after TradeLayer works. The MCP utility's terminal shape — dedicated separately funded account, connect-your-own-LLM, review-before-action — is the harness's current form, on demo data. SigLayer today is a reporting and settlement discipline: scheduled, evidence-based reports read from the chain, with discrepancies carried openly as unconfirmed items until they are resolved — an audit posture, not a trading one. Under its mandate as it stands, no agent trades.

The named next step (awaiting James's tap). A docs-only spec for a trading-hardened harness slice on the existing MCP utility:

  • dynamic position sizing — position size computed from the mandate's parameters, not fixed by habit;
  • take-profit / stop-loss discipline — exits defined when the position opens, enforced by the harness;
  • backtest-before-live-money — no strategy touches live funds until it has run against history in simulation.

The slice is scoped to the dedicated MCP account and to the $100 XSigLayer envelope as its only live-money candidate — the envelope granted 2026-11-09, expiring January 2, 2027, pending activation, with money gated by James through December 2026 in all cases. The spec is a document; filing it spends nothing, trades nothing, and contacts no one.

Why this part exists in this document. Because the pillars demand it — Pillar 1 is a trading pillar, and the harness gap is real. But the priority is stated without hedging: this is not the ultimate focus. Trading is the domain the wider market is racing to build; it is not where this ecosystem's enterprise value concentrates. That is Part 2.

Part 2 — Digital Asset Treasury — the ultimate focus

What this domain is. An entity — a company, a fund, a practice, an operation of any size — holds balances it does not currently need: seasonal cash flow that will be spent in a later quarter, operating reserves above the working line, fiat balances sitting idle between receipt and deployment. Those balances are an asset doing nothing. Digital Asset Treasury is the discipline of putting them to work — optimizing yield on seasonal or non-required cash-flow and fiat balances — under mandates the entity sets, with records the entity can audit. It is not trading: no positions are taken against a market view, no risk tolerance is set against price movement. The questions are a treasurer's questions: how much is truly idle, for how long, at what yield, with what proof, and how fast can it come back?

The enterprise offer. The agentic surface turns that discipline into a service an enterprise can buy:

  • Idle-balance yield optimization as a service. The entity's balances are assessed for what is genuinely non-required and for how long; allocations are proposed against that assessment; yield is earned on balances that would otherwise sit still. The entity's operating cash is never the subject — only the surplus above it, only for the window it is surplus.
  • Agents do the watching; the mandate does the deciding. Agents on this surface monitor balances continuously — inflows, seasonal patterns, upcoming obligations — and propose allocations as the picture changes. Execution happens only inside a gated mandate the entity set in advance: which balances, what ceiling, what instruments, what return-of-funds terms. The three-layer gating of §3.3 applies unchanged — pre-authorization, enforcement before execution, vertical gates — and every action is a chronicle event: proposed, approved by the mandate, executed, settled, reported. The entity can audit any movement after the fact and reconstruct why it happened.
  • Settlement of record: USDC on Solana, per the rail law (§3.6) — the default rail, with the cascade behind it. Treasury movements settle on the same law as everything else in the ecosystem; there is no special treasury rail to explain to an auditor.
  • The treasury home (proposal, unratified). The 2026-11-07 direction proposes Altitude as the treasury home — the level at which SigLayer, TradeLayer, and the test door sit, reserved to the UZE team. It is recorded here as what it is: a proposal, not a ratified decision, and the treasury design does not depend on it to be described.
  • The audit model is SigLayer's reporting discipline. SigLayer's practice — scheduled reports at fixed times, balances read from the chain rather than asserted, every discrepancy carried openly as an unconfirmed item until evidence resolves it, reports delivered in full — is the standard a treasury client is owed. An enterprise does not hand its idle balances to a system that reports vibes; it hands them to a system whose reporting it has already watched behave correctly when the numbers were uncomfortable.

Why this is the enterprise-innovation play. §2.3 framed the surface as optimizing enterprise innovation, and this is where the frame cashes out. An enterprise's product teams innovate on what the enterprise sells; its treasury, in most organizations, innovates on nothing — balances wait, idle, because working them safely used to require attention no one could spare. Agents change that arithmetic: the watching is continuous, the mandate is set once and enforced mechanically, and the record is complete. Enterprises innovate when their treasury works as hard as their product teams — when the balance sheet's quiet corners are managed with the same discipline as its loud ones, and the yield on money that was doing nothing funds the next experiment. That is also why this domain, and not trading, is the focus: treasury is a service every enterprise needs, buyable on evidence — reporting quality, gating rigor, audit completeness — that this ecosystem is already building for its own reasons. Fundio's filed direction points the same way: its Enterprise Services level (payroll, payouts, the Command & Control console) already names a self-directed Web3 treasury for business partners as a later extension. The treasury domain is where the agentic surface meets that enterprise offer.

Honesty box. No treasury product is live today. No enterprise is being served, no balances are being optimized, no yield is being earned by any client under this design. What exists is the substrate — identity, gating, chronicle, metering, settlement law, and a reporting discipline with a track record — and this design for the service that substrate makes possible. UZD plays no part in it: UZD is a reward only, no ledger for it exists, and treasury settlement is USDC on Solana under the rail law. Anything beyond this paragraph requires James's tap and, in its own time, the licensing and compliance work a treasury service owed to enterprises demands.
