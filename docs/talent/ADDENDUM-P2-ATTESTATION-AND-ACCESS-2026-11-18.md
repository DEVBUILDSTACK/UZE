## 1. The assessment is built per vertical × role

A frontier agent builds the **assessment spec** for each configured
role — technical or not. A hospitality role is assessed as
hospitality; a finance role as finance. The assessment's content is
a function of **the vertical** and **the job role description**,
nothing generic.

Worked example of record (James): an H&I CRE financial-underwriting
role of the kind D5 holds — the assessment ascertains property
financial underwriting skills for private-equity outreach:
financial statement analysis, corporate business valuations, and
DealFi-style acquisition screens (annual revenues over a threshold;
accumulated EBITDA for a quarter; run-rate per annum for a startup;
or a single property's NOI against market comps).

## 2. Work history is an input — and becomes an attestation layer

experience**, tiered by education —

## 3. Access economics — the configuring employer pays for access

Access to the assessment builder is gated and paid, for **human and
agentic** configuring employers alike:

1. **Identity first** — the employer completes **KYC / KYB**, as a
   client or as an affiliate.
2. **Then payment** — access is obtained by **paying via x402**
   and/or **staking the NEAR native token**.

**Two-rate posture.** An employer who is an **affiliate before
becoming a client** receives the **affiliate rate**. One who comes
in directly as a client pays the client rate — and is **offered**
the affiliate path, so the rate difference itself incentivizes and
engages them toward affiliation.

This access layer is distinct from fulfillment billing (the Path A
markup / Path B placement fee in the parent spec): access pays for
building and running assessments; fulfillment pays for the talent.
